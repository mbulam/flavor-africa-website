Nairobi’s New Sound

It isn’t one genre. That’s the whole point.

The Flavor Cover · Issue 01 · October 2026

Young city, restless earsWho’s holding the aux

In June 2026, listeners aged 18 to 24 made up 53.7% of every Spotify stream in Nairobi, ahead of Lagos (44.4%) and Johannesburg (29.9%). Even between 2am and 5am, they account for 55% of what the city plays. Whatever Gen Z presses play on, Nairobi hears.

Genge DNA, new clothesStill running the streets

Arbantone takes its name from “urban” and its bones from genge, laid over old Jamaican riddims and delivered in Sheng. Gody Tennor and Tipsy Gee helped it take over, its songs are made for TikTok dance challenges, and in 2026 the club mixes are still full of it, with Toxic Lyrikali among the names on repeat.

The fastest riserKingston, by way of Eastlands

Dancehall was the fastest-growing genre among Kenyan listeners aged 18 to 24 in Spotify’s June 2026 data, up 95% on the year before. Bongo Flava followed at 75%. Gengetone, written off more than once, still grew 48%, and one of its biggest names, Mejja, opened a new chapter this year with his album “Mtoto wa Khadija”.

Slow it downThe Caribbean, slowed down

Not everything is fast. Bien and Tanzania’s Alikiba leaned into zouk on “Finale”, part of a Caribbean wave on East African airwaves. Kodong Klan, the seven-member collective that includes Bensoul and Okello Max, took it further with konpa, the Haitian sound, on “Your Body”.

The plot twistNobody saw this coming

Here’s the twist in the data: gospel grew 37% among young Kenyan listeners, roughly six times faster than drill at 6%. The same generation running the club mixes is quietly making room for the choir.

One to watchFour o’clock tea, all year

Ywaya Tajiri is one half of Watendawili and a member of the Kodong Klan collective. He released “Chai ya saa kumi” in February. By June it was Nairobi’s most-streamed song, and in October it’s still on Kenya’s charts.

On repeat right nowOctober’s main character

If October has a name, it’s Kapitani. “Shing”, his link-up with Toxic Lyrikali, went straight into Kenya’s charts this month, sitting alongside his own “Kitemeo” and “Safe” with Countree Hype. Several songs, one artist, all at once.

So what is the new sound?The new sound is all of it

The old guard isn’t being pushed out. Sauti Sol was still the most-streamed Kenya-connected act in Nairobi in June, and still growing. This month Bien signed with Quality Control, the Atlanta label behind Migos and Lil Baby. Nairobi isn’t picking a lane. It’s playing all of them.
