Fela Kuti and Sade enter the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame

Two Nigerian-born legends were honoured on the same night in Los Angeles, and Fela became the first African-born solo artist in the Hall.

The Wrap · Continental · 15 Nov 2026 · 4 min read

African music had one of its biggest nights at the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame on Saturday. At the Peacock Theater in Los Angeles on 14 November, which was already Sunday morning in Kenya, Fela Kuti and Sade were inducted as part of the Hall’s Class of 2026.

Fela, the Nigerian bandleader, saxophonist and activist who created Afrobeat, received the Early Influence Award. He is now the first African-born solo artist in the Hall. Sade went in through the performer category as a band: Sade Adu with Stuart Matthewman, Paul Denman and Andrew Hale.

The familyThe Kutis carry the night

Fela died in 1997, so his induction was marked with a video tribute, and his family filled the gap. His sons Femi and Seun, his daughter Yeni and his grandson Made were all at the ceremony to receive the honour for him.

A few hours before the show, Femi posted a photo of the family dressed for the evening in West Hollywood, with the caption “Ready for the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame induction ceremony tonight! Congratulations Fela!”

The Early Influence Award honours artists whose music and performance style helped shape rock and roll. Fela built Afrobeat from highlife, jazz, funk and Yoruba rhythms, and gave it politically charged lyrics. Songs like “Water No Get Enemy,” “Zombie” and “Let’s Start” still turn up in DJ sets and samples around the world.

Fela’s yearA long road, a big year

The honour took time. Fela was nominated in 2021 and again in 2022 before getting in on his third try.

It also caps a big run for his legacy. On 31 January 2026, the Recording Academy gave him its Lifetime Achievement Award, the first African artist to receive it, and his children accepted it on his behalf. In 2025, his 1976 album Zombie became the first Nigerian album inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame, and it turns 50 this year.

SadeSade, in her own way

Sade has always kept a famously low profile, and the night stayed true to that. A few days before the ceremony, the band said on their official website that they would not attend in person, and thanked the people who brought them here.

Being inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame is both a gift and a timely reminder to give thanks. Not only to those who voted for us but also every single person who lets our music into their lives.

Sade, in a statement on the band’s website

They also thanked their longtime manager, Roger Davies, and shared the honour with Mike Pela, the producer they called “the fifth Sade,” who died in 2022. “This is as much yours as ours, Mike. Forever our soul mate.”

On the night, singer Andra Day inducted the band and Sienna Spiro performed “Smooth Operator” in their honour. It was Sade’s second nomination; the first came in 2024.

A note on firstsIs Fela the first African in the Hall?

Not quite, and the detail matters. Many reports have called Fela the first African inductee. The more accurate description is the first African-born solo artist. Freddie Mercury, who was born in Stone Town, Zanzibar (now part of Tanzania), was inducted as a member of Queen in 2001.

The classThe rest of the Class of 2026

Fela and Sade were part of an 18-name class. The other performers were Phil Collins, Billy Idol, Iron Maiden, Joy Division/New Order, Oasis, Luther Vandross and Wu-Tang Clan. Celia Cruz, Queen Latifah, MC Lyte and Gram Parsons joined Fela in the Early Influence category. Linda Creed, Arif Mardin, Jimmy Miller and Rick Rubin received the Musical Excellence Award, and Ed Sullivan received the Ahmet Ertegun Award.

Oprah Winfrey inducted the late Luther Vandross, and Queen Latifah and MC Lyte performed a medley that included “U.N.I.T.Y.” and “Ruffneck.”

How to watchCatch it on 8 December

The ceremony was not streamed live. It airs on ABC on 8 December and will stream on Disney+ after that.
