Pete Edochie dies at 79

The actor who became Okonkwo in Things Fall Apart, and went on to play Nollywood's kings, chiefs and fathers for almost forty years, died in Enugu after a long illness.

The Wrap · Continental · 25 Nov 2026 · 4 min read

Chief Pete Edochie has died at the age of 79. His family says he died at about 12:30am on Wednesday 25 November 2026 in Enugu, which was 2:30am in Nairobi, after a long battle with prostate cancer. His son, Leo Pete Edochie, shared the news in a family statement on Instagram that opened with four words: “An iroko has fallen.”

The statement remembered him as a husband, father, brother and kinsman from Akamnato, Ifite Nteje, in Anambra State. He is survived by his wife, the lawyer Josephine Edochie, his children, among them the actor Yul Edochie, and his siblings. The family says funeral arrangements will be announced later.

There was a reason people waited to hear it from the family. Edochie had been falsely reported dead again and again over the years, most recently in February, when Leo dismissed a viral post claiming he had died. This time the news came from the family itself.

The voiceBefore the camera

Long before he was an actor, Edochie was a broadcaster. He joined the Eastern Nigeria Broadcasting Corporation in Enugu in 1967, at 20, starting as a junior programmes assistant and rising to director of programmes. The distinctive voice that later carried so many of his film roles was trained on the radio first.

The roleBecoming Okonkwo

His breakthrough came in 1987, when he played Okonkwo in the Nigerian Television Authority’s adaptation of Chinua Achebe’s Things Fall Apart. It made him famous across Nigeria, and for a generation of viewers it settled what Achebe’s proud, doomed hero looked and sounded like. The role also gave him the name Ebubedike, which stayed with him for the rest of his life.

When the home video era took off in the 1990s, he became one of its most familiar faces, appearing in more than 200 films, among them Rituals and Igodo. He was cast again and again as a king, a chief or a father, and his presence alone told audiences who held the power in a story. Critics sometimes called it typecasting. The industry kept calling him anyway.

He also crossed over to the newer Nollywood. In Genevieve Nnaji’s Lionheart (2018), which Netflix picked up, he played Chief Ernest Obiagu, the owner of a transport company whose health scare sets the whole story moving.

The honoursA national figure

In 2003, President Olusegun Obasanjo made him a Member of the Order of the Niger, one of Nigeria’s national honours. He also received the Industry Merit Award from the Africa Magic Viewers’ Choice Awards and a lifetime achievement award from the Africa Film Academy.

Tributes began within hours of the announcement. Enugu State governor Peter Mbah called him a titan and an icon, and described his death as a personal loss to the state.

For anyone who watched Nigerian television in the late 1980s, Okonkwo has had one face for almost forty years. It still does.
