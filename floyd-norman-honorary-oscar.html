Floyd Norman receives an honorary Oscar

Disney's first Black animator was honoured at the Academy's 17th Governors Awards. He is only the fourth animator ever to receive the award.

The Wrap · Global · 17 Nov 2026 · 4 min read

Floyd Norman, the animator who joined Walt Disney Studios in 1956 as its first Black animator, received an honorary Oscar on Sunday, 15 November 2026. The award was presented at the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences' 17th Governors Awards, held at the Ray Dolby Ballroom at Ovation Hollywood in Los Angeles.

Norman is 91. He is only the fourth animator to receive an honorary Oscar, after Walter Lantz, Chuck Jones and Hayao Miyazaki.

He was honoured on the same night as actor Glenn Close and director Ridley Scott, who also received honorary Oscars, and producers Christine Vachon and Pamela Koffler, who received the Irving G. Thalberg Memorial Award. The Academy first announced the honourees on 10 June.

The speechA thank you to Walt

In his acceptance speech, Norman looked back to where it started, and thanked Walt Disney, the man who hired him, as the best mentor a filmmaker could ever have.

The careerFrom Sleeping Beauty to Toy Story 2

Norman was born in Santa Barbara, California, on 22 June 1935. He was 21 when Disney hired him. His first job was as an in-betweener on Sleeping Beauty, drawing the frames that sit between the lead animators' key poses.

After Sleeping Beauty he was drafted into the military. He came back to the studio and worked on One Hundred and One Dalmatians, The Sword in the Stone and Mary Poppins, then moved up to the story team on The Jungle Book, where he worked under Walt Disney's direct supervision.

Walt Disney died in 1966. Norman then left the studio and co-founded Vignette Films with fellow animator Leo Sullivan. It was one of the first studios to make animated films about Black history.

He went on to work at Hanna-Barbera, and later at Disney Publishing, where he wrote and drew the daily Mickey Mouse comic strip. In the 1990s and 2000s his name turned up on a new generation of hits: Mulan, Toy Story 2 and Monsters, Inc.

The honoursSeventy years in the making

Disney named him a Disney Legend in 2007. He has also received a lifetime achievement honour from the Annie Awards and has been inducted into the Society of Illustrators Hall of Fame. His life was the subject of the 2016 documentary Floyd Norman: An Animated Life.

Now there's an Oscar on the shelf too, seventy years after he first walked into the studio.
